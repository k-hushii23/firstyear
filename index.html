04 · The Four PillarsManifesto

tap a pillar to read the full text

Preparation should begin before the competition does. The idea behind Plan is simple: Rajputana should not have to build teams when the deadline is already around the corner. Participation becomes stronger when people know about opportunities early, have time to prepare, and know where they fit. Start early Competitions should be communicated well in advance so interested students have enough time to prepare rather than being approached at the last minute. Build a wider participant pool Batch-wise and area-wise points of contact can help identify students interested in sports, cultural activities and other competitions. This makes it easier to know who wants to participate and where their strengths lie. Prepare consistently Preparation isn't about studying or practising for hours just for the sake of it. It is about being consistent, knowing what the competition demands, and using the available time intelligently. Turn small efforts into meaningful results A competitive culture is built through regular effort—not through one intense push immediately before an event..

—------------- Plan early. Prepare consistently. Compete with intent.

Leadership is not just about making decisions. It is about taking responsibility for them. A clan leader has to be able to communicate, make decisions, listen to people and also accept when something could have been handled better. Take responsibility Every decision comes with responsibility. Whether it concerns preparation, participation or team decisions, accountability should remain with the person making the decision. Lead by example Punctuality, discipline, reliability and participation should not be expectations placed only on others. Leadership should demonstrate those same standards. Support the people around you A leader should make it easier for teammates to contribute—not make everything dependent on one person. The goal is to create an environment where people can take ownership of their responsibilities. Learn from what doesn't work Not every decision will work perfectly. When something goes wrong, the response should not be to find someone to blame. It should be to understand what happened, take responsibility where necessary, and improve the process.

—------------- Leadership isn't about being right every time. It's about being accountable every time.

Build a Clan People Feel Part Of. A clan should feel like more than a name attached to a competition. Belonging comes from the small things—knowing people across batches, celebrating someone's achievement, cheering during a competition, sharing a joke after a loss, and knowing that your contribution is noticed. Bring people together Create more opportunities for interaction between batches and different groups instead of having people interact only when a competition requires it. Make participation visible Someone doesn't have to win a trophy to contribute to the clan. Showing up, preparing, improving and representing Rajputana are all forms of participation worth recognising. Make new members feel included A new student shouldn't experience Rajputana only when a team needs one more person. Early interaction and involvement can help people feel like members of the clan from the beginning. Build shared experiences Small activities, rivalries, banter, celebrations and even losses can become the memories that make people feel connected to something bigger than themselves.

—------------- Belonging is built when people feel that their presence matters.

Bring Pride to Rajputana. Competition should have purpose. Representing Rajputana means more than simply entering an event. It means preparing properly, giving your best, respecting your teammates and opponents, and carrying the identity of the clan with pride. Give your best Every competition deserves genuine effort, whether it is a major event or a smaller activity. Make selection fair and clear Different competitions demand different qualities. Selection should therefore consider what the particular competition requires, while keeping the process transparent and giving interested students a genuine opportunity to demonstrate their potential. Learn from every competition A result is only one part of a competition. After every event, there should be something to learn—what worked, what didn't, and what can be improved next time. Represent with sportsmanship Being competitive and being respectful are not opposites. The way we behave while winning, losing and competing is also part of how we represent Rajputana.

—------------- Compete hard. Represent proudly. Learn every time.

More Participation. Stronger Rajputana.
